The Core Problem

Most blockchains were designed as
general-purpose computers first.

Payments were treated as a secondary use case.

This is the root issue. Ethereum and the majority of L2s optimize for general computation. When you try to run high-volume, low-value, predictable payments on them, structural friction appears.

What Usually Breaks

flowchart TD A[High Payment Volume] --> B[Shared Blockspace] B --> C[Congestion] C --> D[Unpredictable Fees] C --> E[Delayed Payments]

Payments compete with everything else for the same blockspace

When payments share blockspace with complex DeFi, NFT mints, or heavy computation, congestion directly hurts payment traffic. Fees become unpredictable and inclusion can be delayed.

Additional Structural Problems

flowchart LR F[Volatile Gas Token] --> G[Difficult unit economics] H[Probabilistic Finality] --> I[Settlement uncertainty]

Volatile gas and uncertain finality create real friction for payment systems

Two more important problems: 1. Paying gas in a volatile asset makes costs hard to predict and hard to budget. 2. Probabilistic finality means you never have the same settlement certainty that traditional payment systems expect.

Tempo’s Thesis

A payments-first Layer 1,
purpose-built for stablecoin payments at scale.

Incubated by Stripe and Paradigm

Tempo starts from the opposite direction. Instead of taking a general-purpose chain and trying to make payments work on it, it was designed for payments first while still remaining fully programmable. The combination of Stripe’s payments expertise and Paradigm’s systems expertise is deliberate.

Five Foundational Decisions

  1. No native gas token
  2. Fees paid in USD stablecoins
  3. Deterministic, sub-second finality
  4. Dedicated payment lanes
  5. Full EVM compatibility
These five decisions shape almost every other part of the protocol. We will now examine each one with a simple diagram so the ideas stick.

1. No Native Gas Token

flowchart LR subgraph Traditional["Traditional"] U1[User] --> G[Must hold volatile gas token] G --> TX1[Send transaction] end subgraph TempoChain["Tempo"] U2[User] --> S[Holds only stablecoins] S --> TX2[Send transaction] end Traditional ~~~ TempoChain

Users never need a volatile asset just to pay for gas

On most chains a user must acquire and manage a volatile gas token. On Tempo the user only needs stablecoins. This removes a significant source of friction and risk, especially for businesses and autonomous agents.

2. Stablecoin Gas + Fee AMM

flowchart LR User["User pays with any USD TIP-20"] --> AMM[Fee AMM] AMM --> Val["Validator receives preferred stablecoin"]

The protocol converts stablecoins automatically

The user can pay gas using different USD-denominated TIP-20 tokens. An enshrined Fee AMM converts the token into whatever the current validator prefers to receive. From the user’s perspective the experience is simple.

3. Deterministic Finality

flowchart TD subgraph MostChains["Most Chains"] B1[Block] --> B2[Additional confirmations] B2 --> B3[Reorg still possible] end subgraph TempoFinality["Tempo"] T1[Block produced] --> T2[Finalized in ~0.5s] T2 --> T3[No reorg possible] end

Simplex BFT provides fast, deterministic finality

Tempo uses Simplex BFT. Blocks are produced roughly every 0.5–0.6 seconds and reach deterministic finality quickly. Once a block is finalized it cannot be reorganized. This gives the kind of settlement certainty that real payment systems require.

4. Payment Lanes

flowchart TD subgraph OneBlock["One Block"] PL["Payment Lane - Reserved for payments"] GL[General Lane] end Pay[Payment transactions] --> PL Other[Other transactions] --> GL

Payments receive dedicated blockspace even under congestion

This is one of the most important protocol-level guarantees. A significant portion of blockspace is reserved for payment transactions. Even when the network is under heavy load from complex contracts, payments still have room.

5. Full EVM Compatibility

flowchart LR Sol[Solidity] --> Tools[Foundry / Hardhat / Remix] Tools --> Tempo[Tempo] Tempo --> EVM[Standard EVM execution]

Existing Solidity knowledge and tooling transfer directly

Despite the payment-specific design choices, Tempo remains fully EVM compatible. You can continue using the same languages and tools you already know. The new surface area is mainly in TIP-20, Tempo Transactions, policies, and the fee system.

Putting It Together

flowchart TB User[User or Agent] --> TIP20[TIP-20 Stablecoins] TIP20 --> TT[Tempo Transaction] TT --> PL[Payment Lane] PL --> Final[Finalized in ~0.5s] AMM[Fee AMM] -.-> TT

High-level flow of a payment on Tempo

Here is the combined picture. A user or agent holds TIP-20 stablecoins. They submit a Tempo Transaction. The transaction uses the payment lane. It reaches fast deterministic finality. The Fee AMM handles gas conversion in the background.

Core Mental Model

Most chains optimize for general computation
and then try to support payments.

Tempo optimizes for payments
and still supports general computation.

This is the central shift in thinking. Once this inversion is clear, the rest of the protocol design becomes much easier to reason about.
