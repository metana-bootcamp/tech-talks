TIP-20

Tempo’s Native Token Standard for Payments

Welcome. Today we go deep into TIP-20. This is one of the most important protocol primitives on Tempo. Almost everything else — fees, payment lanes, policies, the enshrined DEX — builds on top of it.

Why TIP-20 Exists

ERC-20 was designed as a general fungible token standard.

TIP-20 is designed specifically for stablecoins and payment flows.

ERC-20 is excellent as a general standard, but it lacks native support for several things that real payment systems need: memos, predictable fee payment, compliance controls, and tight integration with payment-specific blockspace. TIP-20 keeps the familiar ERC-20 interface while adding the missing primitives.

Core Idea

flowchart LR ERC20[ERC-20 Interface] --> TIP20[TIP-20] TIP20 --> Fees[Fee Payment] TIP20 --> Memos[Transfer Memos] TIP20 --> Lanes[Payment Lanes] TIP20 --> Policies[TIP-403 Policies] TIP20 --> Controls[Issuer Controls]

TIP-20 extends ERC-20 with payment-native capabilities

Think of TIP-20 as ERC-20 plus the features that payments actually require. The transfer and balance interface remains familiar. The new capabilities are what make the rest of the Tempo stack work cleanly.

TIP-20 vs ERC-20

Aspect ERC-20 TIP-20
Interface Standard Compatible
Fee payment No Yes (USD TIP-20)
Transfer memos No Yes
Payment lanes No Yes
Admin model Often Ownable Role-based
Compliance hooks Custom Native via TIP-403
The interface is deliberately familiar so existing knowledge transfers. The differences are concentrated in the areas that matter for payments and regulated stablecoins.

Feature: Fee Payment

flowchart LR User[User holds USD TIP-20] --> Tx[Sends transaction] Tx --> Fee[Fee paid in same or other USD TIP-20] Fee --> AMM[Fee AMM] AMM --> Validator[Validator]

Any USD-denominated TIP-20 can be used to pay gas

This is one of the most important capabilities. Only TIP-20 tokens can pay transaction fees on Tempo. Because there is no native gas token, the ability of TIP-20 tokens to serve as fee tokens is fundamental to the whole design.

Feature: Transfer Memos

  • Short onchain reference attached to a transfer
  • Useful for invoices, reconciliation, and payment references
  • Mirrors traditional payment systems more closely than plain ERC-20
In traditional finance almost every payment carries a reference. TIP-20 makes this a first-class feature. When you send a transfer you can attach a memo that is recorded with the transfer. This is extremely useful for businesses and for any system that needs to match onchain payments to offchain records.

Feature: Payment Lanes

flowchart TD subgraph Block PL[Payment Lane
TIP-20 transfers] GL[General Lane] end TIP20Tx[TIP-20 payment tx] --> PL OtherTx[Other transactions] --> GL

Eligible TIP-20 transfers receive reserved blockspace

TIP-20 transfers that qualify as payments can use the dedicated payment lanes. This is how Tempo keeps payment traffic predictable even when the network is busy with other activity. The token standard and the blockspace design are tightly linked.

Feature: Issuer Controls

  • Role-based access control instead of a single owner
  • Roles for minting, burning, pausing, and administration
  • Designed for controlled tokens such as stablecoins
Real stablecoins need careful control over minting and burning. TIP-20 uses role-based access control rather than a simple Ownable pattern. This matches the operational needs of issuers more closely.

Feature: Policy Integration

flowchart LR TIP20[TIP-20 Token] --> Registry[TIP-403 Policy Registry] Registry --> Check[Transfer policy check] Check --> Allow[Allow or block transfer]

TIP-20 tokens can enforce compliance rules via TIP-403

TIP-20 integrates natively with the TIP-403 policy system. A token can reference a policy that controls who is allowed to send or receive. This is the foundation for compliance-oriented stablecoin behavior. We will study TIP-403 in more depth in a later session.

Virtual Addresses

Operators can issue unique deposit addresses that still route funds to a master account.

  • Useful for invoices and customer-specific addresses
  • Keeps accounting clean while preserving a single destination
Virtual addresses are another payment-oriented feature. Instead of giving every customer the same deposit address, an operator can issue distinct addresses that still consolidate to one master wallet. This helps with reconciliation and user experience.

How TIP-20 Powers the Stack

flowchart TB TIP20[TIP-20] TIP20 --> Fees[Fee System] TIP20 --> Lanes[Payment Lanes] TIP20 --> DEX[Enshrined Stablecoin DEX] TIP20 --> Policies[TIP-403 Policies] TIP20 --> Payments[Payment UX]

TIP-20 is the common foundation for multiple protocol features

Once you understand TIP-20, many other parts of Tempo become clearer. The fee system depends on it. Payment lanes depend on it. The enshrined DEX is built around it. Policies attach to it. It is the central token primitive of the chain.

Issuance (High Level)

  • New TIP-20 tokens are created via the TIP-20 Factory
  • Issuers receive the appropriate roles
  • Tokens can then be configured with policies and other parameters
We are not going into full issuance mechanics today. The important point is that TIP-20 tokens are created through a canonical factory rather than being ad-hoc ERC-20 deployments. This keeps behavior consistent across the ecosystem.

Implications for Developers

  • You can treat balances and transfers similarly to ERC-20
  • You should actively use memos where references matter
  • You must understand that fee payment depends on TIP-20
  • Compliance and receive rules may apply via policies
From a Solidity developer’s point of view the day-to-day interface feels familiar. The differences show up when you care about fees, payment semantics, reconciliation, and compliance. Those are exactly the areas Tempo optimizes for.

Mental Model

TIP-20 is not just “another token standard.”

It is the token standard that makes Tempo’s payment design possible.

Hold on to this framing. When we later discuss fees, lanes, the DEX, or policies, the conversation will keep coming back to TIP-20.
