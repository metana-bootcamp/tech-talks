Tempo Zones

Private execution environments attached to Mainnet

This session is for developers who already understand Tempo Mainnet, TIP-20, and TIP-403. Zones are not “a privacy mixin on the same chain.” They are separate sequenced chains rooted to Mainnet, with locked funds, validity proofs, and a two-layer privacy model.

What You Should Leave With

  • What a Zone is architecturally — and what it is not
  • The trust split: sequencer sees activity, proofs bind funds
  • Two-layer privacy: execution + RPC. Why one layer is not enough
  • Deposit, encrypted deposit, withdrawal, and callback flows
  • How TIP-20 and TIP-403 are mirrored, not waived
  • What you can and cannot query from a Zone RPC
If someone already knows validiums or private L2s, this should still be worth their time because of the account-scoped RPC, fixed-gas side-channel mitigations, encrypted deposits, and policy mirroring.

Definition

A Zone is a dedicated chain with one sequencer,
rooted to Tempo Mainnet.

  • No zone data is published on Mainnet
  • Balances, transfers, and history are invisible to explorers
  • Each Zone has its own execution and throughput
  • Liquidity is shared through Mainnet, not copied as a new token
Adding Zones increases throughput without congesting Mainnet payment lanes. Funds that enter a Zone are locked on Mainnet in that Zone’s portal. The Zone is not a wrapped-asset island. It is a private execution environment over the same TIP-20 assets.

What a Zone Is Not

  • Not a stealth-address scheme on Mainnet
  • Not a mixer
  • Not “encrypt the calldata and keep the same public state”
  • Not permissionless-private against the sequencer
Be explicit about the threat model. Outside observers — explorers, indexers, other users — see nothing. The sequencer sees everything inside the Zone. Funds cannot be stolen or forged because of proofs, but privacy is not sequencer-private.

Architecture

flowchart TB User[User] --> Main[Tempo Mainnet] Main --> Portal[ZonePortal] Portal --> Seq[Zone sequencer] Seq --> Zone[Zone execution] Seq --> Proof[Validity proof / TEE attestation] Proof --> Portal

Lock on Mainnet. Execute privately. Prove the transition.

User deposits into the portal. Sequencer observes the deposit queue, executes inside the Zone, and later posts a batch with a proof. The portal is the custody and settlement hinge. The Zone is the private VM.

Canonical Addresses

ComponentAddress
ZoneFactory0x5AF2…0000
ZonePortal implementation0x5AD1…0000
Zone verifier0x5a56…0000
ZoneMessenger0x5A4d…0000
T10 made zone creation a native protocol operation. Factory is a precompile. Each zone gets a deterministic portal whose address encodes the zone ID. Do not hand-roll portal address checks in app code. Call ZoneFactory.isZonePortal(address).

Deterministic Identity

chain_id = 421700000 + zone_id

portal ≈ 0x5AD000000000000000000000 || zone_id_be_8_bytes
zone 1 portal = 0x5AD0000000000000000000000000000000000001
4217 is Tempo Mainnet’s chain ID, reused as a prefix. Wallets and RPC clients must treat each Zone as its own EIP-155 chain. Mixing Mainnet chain ID 4217 with a zone RPC is a class of footgun you should warn students about immediately.

Zone Predeploys

  • TempoState — the Zone’s view of Mainnet headers so contracts can read Mainnet state inside proofs
  • Inbox / outbox style bridging surfaces
  • Fixed system contracts; contract creation inside a Zone reverts
Zones are not general-purpose deployment environments in the current design. That is intentional. Privacy plus arbitrary contract creation would explode the leak and proving surface. Think payments, balances, policy-preserving transfers — not “deploy Uniswap in private.”

Trust Split

flowchart LR Seq[Sequencer] --> See[Sees all zone activity] Proof[Validity proof] --> Funds[Cannot steal or forge state]

Privacy depends on the operator. Solvency depends on the proof.

Say this twice if needed. Integrity of funds ≠ privacy from the sequencer. The sequencer orders and executes. The proof says the state transition and queues were correct. If your product promise is “even the operator cannot see this,” Zones as specified today do not give you that.

Proving Model

  • Sequencer submits batches via submitBatch on the portal
  • Batch includes block transition, deposit-queue progress, withdrawal-queue hash
  • Same verifier interface for ZK (SP1) and TEE (SGX/TDX)
  • Proof checks state transition, TempoState commitment, and outbox batch index
The interesting engineering choice is one state-transition function that can run in a ZKVM or a TEE. App developers rarely generate these proofs themselves. They do need to know settlement is batched and asynchronous, and that withdrawals become claimable only after a proven batch.

Two-Layer Privacy

flowchart TB Q[Who can learn a balance?] Q --> E[Execution layer] Q --> R[RPC layer] E --> E1[balanceOf reverts unless self or sequencer] R --> R1[RPC scoped to authorized account]

Either layer alone is bypassable.

This is the sharpest teaching point in the session. Execution alone fails because eth_getStorageAt can read mapping slots. RPC alone fails because eth_call can run a helper contract that reads someone else’s balance and returns it. Both layers exist because each bypasses the other.

Private Balances at Execution

  • balanceOf(account) succeeds if msg.sender == account
  • Succeeds if caller is the sequencer
  • Otherwise reverts Unauthorized()
  • Same idea extends to allowances
Sequencer access is required for block production and fee accounting. Everyone else, including other zone contracts composing through you, should not be able to learn balances. That changes how you write contracts. Public view functions that assume global readable balances are invalid here.

RPC Is Authenticated and Scoped

  • Every request carries X-Authorization-Token
  • Token is signed by a Tempo account key (secp256k1, P256, WebAuthn, or access key)
  • Max lifetime: 1 month
  • Binds spec version, zone ID, chain ID, issued-at, expiry
  • Zone 0 in the token can mean “all zones”
This is not public Ethereum JSON-RPC with an API key taped on. The token proves control of an account and scopes responses to that account. Replay protection is in the signed payload. Do not mint long-lived god tokens for backends.

What RPC Will and Will Not Give You

  • Allowed: chain ID, block number, gas price, headers without txs
  • Headers: transaction bodies omitted, logsBloom zeroed on subscriptions
  • Account-scoped: your deposits, your receipts, your events
  • Filtered out: other users’ activity and most system/config events
If you are building an explorer-style UI against a Zone RPC, you will be disappointed and that is the point. You can build a wallet for one account. You cannot build a global mempool viewer.

Deposit: Mainnet → Zone

flowchart TB D[ZonePortal.deposit] --> Lock[Validate token, lock funds, charge fee] Lock --> Q[Append deposit queue] Q --> S[Sequencer advances inbox] S --> Mint[Mint zone-side TIP-20 to recipient] Mint --> P[Batch proof includes queue progress]

Deposits are queued and proven. They are not a same-block balance update.

Walk this slowly. deposit(token, to, amount, memo) on Mainnet. Fee is deducted from the deposit amount and paid to the sequencer on Mainnet. FIXED_DEPOSIT_GAS is 100,000. Rate is set on the portal. The zone mint happens later when the sequencer processes the queue.

Encrypted Deposits

  • Private on-ramp: Mainnet observers should not learn the zone recipient
  • Invalid encrypted deposits must not halt chain progress
  • Use this when the deposit itself would otherwise leak the destination
Standard deposits leak the zone recipient on Mainnet. Encrypted deposits exist so the on-ramp can stay private. Robustness matters: a garbage encrypted payload cannot brick the deposit queue.

Withdrawal: Zone → Mainnet

  1. User creates a withdrawal request inside the Zone
  2. Sequencer finalizes a withdrawal batch at the end of a proven batch
  3. Proof commits withdrawalQueueHash and batch index
  4. Mainnet later pays out from the portal
Two-step by design. Request in the Zone, settle on Mainnet after proof. Withdrawal fee is gasLimit × tempoGasRate in the withdrawn token. Users pick gasLimit high enough to cover processing and any callback.

Composable Withdrawal Callbacks

flowchart LR Z1[Zone A] --> W[Withdraw to Mainnet] W --> Dex[Optional DEX swap] Dex --> Z2[Deposit into Zone B]

Atomic transfer + callback. If callback reverts, transfer reverts.

This is how cross-zone value movement stays composable without exposing the trader as a public Mainnet flow they have to babysit. Receiving contracts implement IWithdrawalReceiver and must check msg.sender == zoneMessenger. That authentication check is mandatory. Missing it is an integration bug.

TIP-20 and TIP-403 Still Bind

  • Only enabled TIP-20s move into a Zone
  • Issuer policy is mirrored, not stripped
  • Proofs commit that batch execution followed those rules
  • Zone operator enablement and issuer policy are both required
Privacy is not a compliance escape hatch. A blacklisted address does not become spendable because the transfer happened “in private.” If you taught TIP-403 earlier, connect it here explicitly so students do not invent a loophole.

Gas Inside a Zone

  • Same USD TIP-20 fee-token model
  • No Fee AMM inside the Zone — sequencer accepts enabled tokens directly
  • TIP-20 transfer / approve / memo variants are fixed at 100,000 gas
  • Fixed costs remove gas-based occupancy leaks
On public EVM, gas variation on cold vs warm slots can leak whether an address has been used. Zones fix those user-facing TIP-20 ops at 100k so storage occupancy is not a side channel. That is a protocol choice you should name. It is not an implementation accident.

Asynchrony Is the UX Reality

  • Deposit, routed send, swap-across-zones, withdraw all settle in stages
  • A Mainnet tx landing is not the same as the zone balance updating
  • Wallets must model pending portal state, not only local nonce
Demo this if you can. Students coming from Mainnet TIP-20 transfers expect sub-second finality and immediate balances. Zones keep fast proofs as a goal, but the product surface is still staged. If your UI shows “sent” when only the portal lock landed, you will support tickets forever.

Integration Checklist

  1. Use the Zone chain ID, not 4217, on the Zone RPC
  2. Authenticate RPC with a short-lived account-scoped token
  3. Never assume public balanceOf or global logs
  4. Treat deposits and withdrawals as queues plus proofs
  5. Authenticate callbacks with zoneMessenger
  6. Keep TIP-403 in the mental model
  7. Do not promise sequencer-private confidentiality
This is the close for working engineers. Most production bugs will be chain-ID confusion, treating staged settlement as atomic, or leaking via a helper contract / storage read that Mainnet habits make feel normal.

Mental Model

Mainnet is the settlement and liquidity plane.

A Zone is a private execution plane with a trusted-for-privacy sequencer.

Proofs protect funds. Two layers protect account data from everyone else.

Compress the whole session into those three sentences. If they can repeat them, they are ready to read the bridging and RPC specs without getting lost.
